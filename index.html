The work down there

Ocean science starts with getting wet. Divers survey reefs, repair instruments, and count what lives below — one splashy backward roll at a time.

The sunlit garden

Coral reefs cover less than one percent of the ocean floor but shelter a quarter of all marine species. Scientists watch them closely — pale corals are a warning sign the water is too warm.

What the sea keeps

The deep is a museum. Cold, dark, and still, it preserves wooden ships for centuries — this one rests in the Gulf of Mexico, slowly becoming a reef of its own.

Going deeper

Where divers can't go, robots do. Remotely operated vehicles dive for hours in crushing darkness, piloted from a ship far above, sending back video of places no human eye has seen.

What's below

Most of the deep ocean has never been explored. Drifting through the dark like a lantern, this jellyfish is a reminder: the maps aren't finished yet.
